# EasyT business plan

## The one-page version

EasyT helps independent travellers turn a trip idea into a flexible, useful plan they can actually use while travelling. It starts with a route, adapts to the traveller’s preferences, makes pace and travel-time trade-offs visible, then remains useful on the ground through map planning, nearby finders and Stamps.

For the first 12 months, the business should focus on one job: help an independent, mobile-first traveller plan a multi-stop trip without ending up with an overstuffed spreadsheet, a generic itinerary or five disconnected apps.

The first commercial offer should be **EasyT Plus**, a low-friction subscription that unlocks unlimited saved trips, richer planning guidance, exports, offline-friendly trip access and priority local discovery. The free product must be useful enough to create a complete first trip; Plus should make EasyT the tool a traveller keeps using.

The key number is **activated weekly planners**: people who create a trip, personalise it and return to it. Revenue only becomes durable when planning turns into repeat usage.

---

## 1. Think like a business owner

### Solve one problem

**Problem:** Planning a meaningful multi-stop trip is fragmented and mentally expensive. Search results are generic, itineraries are rigid, and the tools people use before departure often stop being useful once they arrive.

**EasyT promise:** *A thoughtful first trip plan that still leaves room for you.*

EasyT is not trying to replace booking sites, a local guide or a traveller’s judgement. It earns its place by helping people make better choices faster, explaining the shape of a trip, and keeping that plan useful in the moment.

### Pick one primary audience

**Primary customer:** English- and Spanish-speaking independent travellers, roughly 25–45, planning complex, multi-stop international trips in Asia, South America or Europe. They value food, culture, nature and flexibility; they use their phone while travelling; they are willing to pay to remove planning stress.

**Duration posture:** Treat roughly 1–12 weeks as an exploratory supported range, from a week-long multi-city break to a multi-month journey. Duration alone is not a customer qualifier or a reason to reject a viable trip. A likely 2–6 week complexity sweet spot is an internal hypothesis to validate through research, not a proven or public claim.

**Early use case:** “I have a rough idea of where I want to go, but I need a realistic route and want the freedom to adjust it.”

**Do not target first:** business travel, all-inclusive holidays, group tour operators, luxury concierge travel or every possible destination. Those can come later if the core loop works.

### Create one offer

| Tier | Purpose | Suggested price |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free | Build, edit and use one active trip. Show the product’s planning advantage. | Free |
| EasyT Plus | Unlimited saved trips, exports, advanced pacing checks, richer map planning, saved finder results and full Stamps memories. | Test US$6–9/month or US$39–59/year |
| Route packs, later | Paid, high-quality editorial routes or specialist collaborations. | Test US$9–19 each |

The annual plan should be the main paid choice because travel planning is seasonal. Do not put the first useful itinerary behind a paywall.

### Know the big why

Travel should feel more personal than a booking funnel. EasyT should make the difficult part, deciding what to do with limited time, feel calmer and more human.

## 2. Plan what matters

### Funnel

```text
Discover a route or trip idea
        ↓
Start a trip from the homepage
        ↓
Choose dates, places and pace
        ↓
Open the editable itinerary/map
        ↓
Return to adjust, save or use nearby
        ↓
Create an account and keep the trip
        ↓
Upgrade when repeat use needs more depth
```

### One traffic source at a time

Start with **searchable editorial route pages plus short-form social content** pointing to those routes. The landing pages should answer real trip-intent questions, then let a traveller personalise the route immediately.

Examples:

- Japan in 10 days without rushing
- Portugal: Lisbon, Comporta and Lagos in one week
- Peru’s Sacred Valley at a slower pace
- Taiwan by train for food and tea

Each piece needs one clear action: **Open the route** or **Start my version**. Do not send early users to a generic marketing page.

### Growth loops

1. **Route-to-trip loop:** a useful route becomes an editable personal trip.
2. **Trip-to-share loop:** travellers share a lightweight public itinerary with friends or travel companions.
3. **Memory loop:** completed trips become Stamps and highlights, encouraging a return before the next trip.
4. **Discovery loop:** a traveller who finds a useful nearby restaurant or stay sees EasyT as a companion, not just a planner.

### Three weekly wins

Every week, choose only three outcomes:

1. Improve one step in the activation funnel.
2. Publish or improve one high-intent route/discovery page.
3. Speak to or observe at least five target travellers using the product.

## 3. Track the wins

### Revenue goal

Treat the first goal as learning, not scale: prove that 10 people will pay and keep using EasyT. Then aim for 100 paying annual-equivalent subscribers before spending meaningfully on paid acquisition.

### The key number

**Activated weekly planners** = people who, within seven days, create a trip with dates and at least two places, open the itinerary or map, and return on another day.

This is stronger than sign-ups because it measures whether EasyT has become useful.

### Three metrics to track each week

| Metric | Why it matters | Directional early target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visitor → trip started | Is the homepage and route content clear? | 10–20% from high-intent route pages |
| Trip started → activated planner | Does the builder lead to a useful plan? | 35–50% |
| Activated planner → account saved | Is the value worth keeping? | 25–40% |

Add conversion to Plus only after the core activation event is reliable. Track qualitative evidence beside every metric: what people expected, where they hesitated, and what they called valuable.

### Simple dashboard

Use one weekly dashboard with:

- visitors by source;
- route opens;
- trip starts;
- activated planners;
- account saves;
- repeat planners after 7 and 30 days;
- subscription starts and cancellations, once live;
- top five feedback themes.

Use a weekly **stop / start / scale** decision:

- **Stop** work that does not improve activation or teach something important.
- **Start** the smallest experiment that addresses the biggest drop-off.
- **Scale** the route, message or feature that clearly earns repeat use.

## 4. Keep it moving

### Launch fast

The next launch should be a focused beta, not a feature-complete travel platform:

- three polished, varied route families: Asia, South America and Europe;
- a clear homepage-to-builder journey;
- account saving, sharing and the essential emails working reliably;
- map planner, nearby finder and Stamps as the distinguishing companion layer;
- instrumentation for the activation funnel.

Invite 20–40 target travellers. Ask them to plan a real upcoming trip, not a hypothetical one.

### Repeatable systems

- **Route quality system:** a fixed editorial template covering best window, bases, travel time, pace, trade-offs and editable starting plan.
- **Feedback system:** one in-product prompt after a meaningful action; review tags weekly; reply to high-signal feedback.
- **Content system:** one route page → several social posts → one email → one improvement to the route based on real questions.
- **Release system:** every release names the user problem, the expected metric movement and a rollback condition.

### Lifecycle communication

Keep email useful and sparse:

1. Welcome: explain how to build and save the first trip.
2. Trip saved: one clear return link, no generic newsletter copy.
3. Pre-trip: surface the plan, pacing checks and map.
4. During trip: optional, location-aware reminders only if the traveller opts in.
5. After trip: invite a Stamp, a memory and the next route.

## 5. Decide with clarity

### Product principles

1. **A starting point, not a prescription.** Every suggestion stays editable.
2. **Useful before, during and after.** Planning alone is not enough.
3. **Show the trade-off.** Explain pace, travel time and why a suggestion exists.
4. **Mobile first.** The map and day plan must work one-handed and on the move.
5. **Earn trust.** Be explicit when data is estimated, local, editorial or user-provided.

### Prioritisation score

Score potential work 1–5 on:

- impact on activated weekly planners;
- number of target travellers affected;
- evidence from behaviour or feedback;
- effort and maintenance cost;
- strategic differentiation.

Prioritise only work with a clear hypothesis. For example: “Adding a calm-pace warning will increase itinerary opens and reduce abandoned builders.”

### 12-week cycle

| Weeks | Focus | Evidence of progress |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–2 | Instrument funnel, recruit beta travellers, remove reliability blockers | Events work; 20+ beta invites accepted |
| 3–4 | Improve homepage, route discovery and builder activation | More visitors start a real trip |
| 5–6 | Improve itinerary/map return value and sharing | More planners return after day one |
| 7–8 | Test Plus value with a small paywall or paid annual founding plan | First paying users and objections captured |
| 9–10 | Double down on the best route/content source | Repeatable acquisition signal |
| 11–12 | Retention, pricing review and next-cycle decision | Clear stop/start/scale choices |

## 6. Daily game plan

### Weekly cadence

- **Monday:** review metrics, choose the one funnel constraint and three weekly wins.
- **Tuesday–Wednesday:** ship the smallest product or route improvement that addresses it.
- **Thursday:** publish/distribute one useful route story and talk to users.
- **Friday:** review feedback, update the dashboard, decide stop/start/scale.

### Message framework

Lead with a real traveller tension, not “AI travel planning.”

**Headline:** Plan a trip that still has room to happen.

**Proof:** See the route, travel time, pace and nearby options in one editable place.

**Action:** Start with a route, then make it your own.

### Early objections to answer

| Objection | EasyT answer |
| --- | --- |
| “I already use Google Maps.” | Keep using it. EasyT connects the shape of the trip, day plan and saved choices around it. |
| “I do not want another rigid itinerary.” | EasyT deliberately starts with flexible days, editable activities and visible pacing trade-offs. |
| “Can I trust the recommendations?” | Make sources, estimates and limitations visible. Nearby finder results are real mapped places, not invented lists. |
| “Why would I pay?” | Free gets a useful first trip. Plus saves time across multiple trips and keeps the plan useful before, during and after travel. |

---

## 7. Build it

### Next practical decisions

1. Choose the founding-plan price and one annual offer.
2. Add product analytics for the activation events above.
3. Recruit the first 20–40 real-trip beta users.
4. Select three route pages to become the acquisition wedge.
5. Run one paid-value test only after the activation funnel is stable.

### What not to build yet

- A general-purpose AI chat assistant with no trip context.
- Hotel, flight or restaurant booking inventory.
- Collaboration beyond simple sharing.
- Coverage for every destination.
- A social feed.

These may be valuable later, but they dilute the first proof: EasyT helps a traveller create and use a calmer, more personal multi-stop trip.
