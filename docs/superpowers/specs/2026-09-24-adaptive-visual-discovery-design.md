# Adaptive visual Discovery before Builder (#321 expansion)

Status: **design specification for review; no implementation authorised by this document.**
Audited base: `59d92819dfe7e168bd8e727ff2572eeb4c319cbd` (the local cumulative head, four commits after `origin/staging` at audit time). This specification uses the two founder-approved mockups for visual hierarchy and interaction direction only. Their destinations, combinations, times, counts, maps, and imagery are illustrative, not travel evidence.

## 1. Job, boundaries, and audited owners

The traveller's job is to turn a broad or experience-led idea into a small set of understandable choices, then enter Builder with their intent intact and a route they can change. Discovery is one modal journey immediately before Builder's main planning surface. It extends #321's canonical capture, clarification, choice, and Add stop paths; it does not create a second planner, resolver, or data platform.

### Confirmed Australia funnel on this base

The catalogue has four Australian settlement identities: Sydney, Melbourne, Hobart, and Adelaide. None of those catalogue rows contains coordinates. Reviewed route families contribute five stop records across two families: Sydney once in each family, plus Byron Bay, Airlie Beach, and Cairns. That is **nine raw locality records and seven distinct locality names** at source, with Sydney the overlap. The route stops supply coordinates for four names, so `guidedPlanningAreaSuggestions("Australia")` yields **four suggestions**: Sydney, Byron Bay, Airlie Beach, Cairns. Its six-item cap does **not** bind this case. `buildCountryDiscovery` keeps **one eligible, ranked candidate**, Sydney (score 22 in the audited run); the three `route-base:` identities have no corresponding canonical catalogue row. Melbourne, Hobart, and Adelaide never entered the suggestion list because they lacked coordinates. The modal initially displays **one of one**; its four-card and ten-card limits also do **not** cause Sydney-only here. For unknown duration and for a tested 14-night context, the result remained one. The primary defects are incomplete place coverage/coordinates and a canonical-identity filter mismatch. The early six cap and later display limits are separate scale defects that would hide a future 20+ collection if left in place.

Content records need **independent evidence fields**: canonical identity/type/containment and coordinates; visitor relevance and distinctiveness; overnight suitability; attraction/base or camp/gateway access relation; stay guidance; image asset, credit, license, and alt text. Missing evidence yields neutral copy or a search/clarification path. A model may summarize reviewed facts but never become the provenance for a place, route, access statement, or photograph. Reuse `place-catalog`, `destination-knowledge`, `route-catalog`, and `route-images`; enrich their data and narrowly extend schemas only for a relation they cannot express. Do not bulk import unreviewed places just to meet a count.

## 3. Adaptive flow and entry rules

Discovery runs only when the capture has a broad or unresolved decision that materially affects the route. It is a single modal shell with an internal step stack; the traveller can close, resume, or return to the previous decision. It does not trap a traveller who already supplied an actionable, precise route.

1. **Classify intent without overwriting it.** Use existing `ResolvedPlaceMention` type, routability, parent geography, explicit route entries, dates, and commitments. More specific explicit mentions constrain broad parents; a landmark or natural area remains visible after a base is selected. If the input is ambiguous or unsupported, say what is missing and offer contained search.
2. **Direction or place entry.** For a continent/macro-region, show genuinely different supported travel directions, each with its actual geography and evidence. Choosing one narrows browsing; it does not select a country or add all of its stops. For a country, show region/experience directions plus the wider place collection. Australia is the first complete instance. Prior named anchors remain available and cannot be filtered away by a direction.
3. **Progressive place exploration.** Start with a few useful cards; expose group navigation, counts, more results, and contained search. The user can preview, compare, and shortlist without route mutation. Include parks, islands, regions, and landmarks as browsable entities, labelled by type. Where an item cannot be an overnight base, its action is visit or choose a base, not Add overnight stop.
4. **Base/visit decision only where needed.** Taj takes a short landmark-to-base path if Agra or an existing suitable base is supported. Kruger branches into verified inside/outside approaches, then an actual camp or outside base and access review. Lake Atitlán offers stay at a supported base, visit from an existing suitable base, or intentionally split nights between supported bases. If one decision is already clear from explicit input and evidence, omit extra steps.
5. **Review and continue.** Show original intent, chosen direction, shortlisted visits and bases, proposed new stops, existing reused stops, timing/continuity concerns, and what remains undecided. The primary action says what will be added. Confirm through Builder's existing canonical mutation path, then land in Builder with editable route and preserved provenance. The modal's Back control changes only the visible step; it preserves draft choices. Closing saves a resumable draft and leaves unconfirmed route stops untouched.

**Skip rule:** When every requested destination is a validated overnight base or an already linked visit, and the existing route has sufficient explicit ordering/commitments, go directly to Builder. A precise route can still open Discovery intentionally to explore alternatives. Unknown duration alone does not force a duration question; ask only when a claim or commitment depends on it. An invalid/ambiguous precise route uses the existing clarification boundary rather than a generic inspiration carousel.

## 4. Visual and interaction contract

The reference images establish an editorial hierarchy: strong place photography, clear location context, selected-card treatment, and a visible final decision. Reuse Morrovia's live typography, spacing, colour, buttons, loading/status and photo-credit patterns from `journey-design.css`, current Journey surfaces, and Storybook. Do not copy the mockup's travel text or numerical labels. The first step must explain the traveller's actual input, not open with a generic catalogue.

**Desktop:** A generous dialog occupies most of the viewport while preserving a visible backdrop and close control. The same shell holds a step header/progress, an image-led direction or place area, optional adjacent geographic context, a concise explanation of the highlighted choice, and a persistent action/footer region. Map and card selection are synchronised: clicking a pin focuses/previews its card; selecting a card focuses its pin. Pins identify actual canonical items and open a keyboard-accessible preview; decorative maps or unavailable imagery never leave dead pins. A map can be hidden if it adds no useful distinction or fails to load. Map attribution and image credit remain legible.

**Mobile:** Use a full-screen dialog with one vertical content scroll owner, readable cards, and an optional Map view toggle. Cards and map are alternative views of the same highlighted item; switching views does not change the shortlist. Any persistent or sticky action treatment must leave cards unobscured, avoid a tiny nested viewport, and preserve useful browsable content height, with sufficient bottom and safe-area padding. Do not recreate the fixed-footer problem removed from the mobile Map drawer. No nested scroll trap, mandatory drag, tiny hit target, or second modal. Back, Close, and Continue remain easy to reach. Loading, sparse, no-photo, map-error, and unsupported-content states preserve the traveller's context and a meaningful next action.

The existing clarification dialog owns internal Back, Close/Escape, modal focus return, and resume behaviour; closing and reopening restores the last completed step and draft. Discovery v1 does not add browser history entries per step or intercept browser Back/forward; existing page/router behaviour remains unchanged. Announce step and shortlist changes; distinguish keyboard focus, hover/highlight, pressed shortlist, and committed stop states. Honour reduced motion. English and Spanish copy must cover every direction, qualifier, button, empty/error state, type label, credit, and accessibility name. Implementation review compares 320, 390, 430, 768, 1024, 1440, and 1680 px Storybook/live states.

## 5. State, persistence, and handoff contract

State has five distinct layers:

| Layer | Meaning | Owner and mutation boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Highlight | Transient card/pin focus or hovered preview. | Modal view state; never persisted as trip truth and never adds a place. |
| Direction and shortlist | Explicit direction preference and candidate IDs the traveller is considering. | A versioned Discovery draft associated with the existing mention in `StructuredTripBrief`; changing direction retains explicit shortlist items and marks out-of-scope ones for review instead of silently deleting them. No route mutation. |
| Base choice | Canonical overnight place, including reuse of an existing stop. | Draft selection linked to its originating mention; Builder's Add stop boundary commits a new base only after review. Reuse references the existing stop ID and preserves its nights. |
| Visit choice | Landmark/park/natural-area intent plus a chosen compatible base or unresolved base requirement. | Extend/reuse `PlaceSelection` visit relationship and original `placeMentions`; committing the visit does not turn the attraction into an overnight stop. |
| Accepted route stop | Explicit, canonical stop and any protected nights/order. | Existing Builder/trip document and structural undo. Only explicit Continue/confirm or existing direct Add stop action can mutate it. |

Current `countryDiscoveryChoices` is a per-mention array of candidate IDs; absence means defaults and an **empty array is an explicit clear**. It cannot represent a direction, visit/base role, review step, or progressive shortlist. Specify a small optional, versioned `discoveryDraftByMentionId` on `StructuredTripBrief` for those draft fields and stable canonical IDs; keep `countryDiscoveryChoices` readable for old trips until migration is complete. On read, migrate a legacy choice array to shortlist exactly once, including empty arrays; do not re-run defaults over it. On write, use the new draft as authority and avoid competing arrays. Unknown/newer versions fail closed into preserved raw intent and ordinary Builder clarification, not a destructive reset. Add a corresponding optional persistence validator/serializer change and an explicit local recovery test; no database migration is needed if the current JSON brief storage accepts the optional field, but confirm repository validation before implementation. Keep transient highlighted pin and map viewport outside persisted trip truth.

The versioned homepage snapshot/receipt remains the handoff authority before Builder hydration. Discovery draft changes must be durably saved under the same owner/trip identity using existing recovery/storage semantics before announcing them as saved; a blocked save shows the existing recovery feedback and keeps the modal open. After trip hydration, the trip document is authoritative. Reload, tab return, and close/resume restore explicit shortlist/base/visit decisions and the step, then revalidate them against current canonical content without silently replacing or deleting them. Missing content is flagged for review. The modal's Back control changes the visible step only and preserves direction, shortlist, base, and visit decisions. Those decisions change only through explicit actions such as Add, Remove, Change direction, Change base, or Reset; committed route changes use Builder's structural undo. Explicit removals stay removed. A parent mention is complete only on explicit confirmation, and the original landmark/area remains recorded after base choice. Confirm is idempotent by mention/canonical selection and must reuse an existing stop instead of adding a duplicate or extra nights. Partial async failure leaves the parent open and shows which items succeeded; retry skips already committed items.

## 6. Recommendation, routing, and scope

Rank browsable content with reviewed relevance, explicit anchors/interests, geography, diversity, and known constraints. Keep browsing broad; a conservative proposed route is a separate output. Recommendation labels have defined meanings: **fits the current plan** requires existing time and route evidence with no known conflict; **worth considering** has visitor evidence but incomplete fit; **substantially changes the trip** conflicts with an explicit route/region or demands a major route change. If duration is unknown, never use a time-fit label or invented day range. If evidence is sparse, expose that limitation and ask for only the next material constraint. For broad Africa, a one-country default may be a cautious candidate route, but the direction gallery must still offer genuinely distinct supported choices without silently selecting one.

Use existing route-candidate generation, country-continuity scoring, trip validation, and night allocation for the review and subsequent Builder. Preserve fixed dates, explicit order, must-visit anchors, manual nights, and the traveller's removals. Review may say that a route needs checking; it must not present visual map proximity as proof of access, transfers, park entry, or feasible nights. A confirmed direction is a preference and potential source of proposed stops, not an instruction to inject a route-family itinerary. No new optimiser, automatic flight/drive assumption, or generated factual claim is in scope.

### Australia-first implementation slice to specify for the next plan

Complete **broad Australia idea → supported directions/regions → 20+ progressively accessible, distinct canonical places → shortlist with base/visit types → review → Builder**, using the common owner/state model above. The 20+ is a content acceptance threshold, not an instruction to display or preselect 20 cards. Every displayed destination/place must have canonical identity, valid geography and containment, and reviewed visitor-relevance evidence. A place may be labelled browse/explore-only when route fit, overnight suitability, or access evidence is incomplete; it cannot be committed as an overnight base or route stop until the required evidence exists. Do not add weakly sourced places to reach the threshold. Remove the six-item upstream cap for the discovery collection, retain bounded progressive pages/groups, and preserve a separately limited route proposal. Curate photos/credits or designed fallbacks. Validate old #321 choices and homepage/new-trip resumes through the new handoff. Storybook should cover directions, dense collection, selected/reused item, sparse/no-photo, map-error, mobile full-screen, and English/Spanish states. This section defines scope, not an implementation plan or file-by-file task list.

Then validate the same architecture, without separate state systems, against Africa's direction-first branch, Taj's short landmark/base branch, Kruger's verified inside/outside access branch, Lake Atitlán's stay/visit/split branch, and the precise-route skip. Publish those branches only as their coverage and relationships become supportable; keep their existing clarification/search fallbacks in the meantime.

## 7. Acceptance scenarios and risks

1. **Australia, no duration:** at least 20 distinct places with canonical identity, valid geography/containment, and reviewed visitor relevance across multiple geography/experience groups are accessible progressively; a small starting view and no blanket preselection; no time-fit statement. A browse/explore-only place with incomplete route fit, overnight suitability, or access evidence cannot become an overnight base or route stop. Choosing a direction then several places changes only the draft. Review names the proposed bases/visits and existing stops. Confirm adds only selected, supported stops and opens Builder. Reload and the modal's Back control preserve explicit choices.
2. **Australia with a constrained route:** the wider collection remains browsable, but suggestions that would materially change fixed plans are labelled accordingly. Existing stops are reused, with no duplicate night allocation. An explicit deselection stays deselected after reload.
3. **Africa:** the first decision exposes multiple actually supported directions; none is silently assigned from catalogue density. Selecting one previews contained options; cross-country feasibility is deferred to existing route review. A sparse region has an honest fallback.
4. **Taj Mahal:** the landmark survives capture, a supported Agra/existing-base option can produce a linked visit, and the traveller can reach Builder without a continent-style flow. No unsupported Delhi transfer claim is shown.
5. **Kruger:** park, camp, gateway, and reserve have distinct identities. Inside/outside claims require reviewed access evidence; otherwise the UI asks for a suitable base and keeps the park intent. No straight-line access promise or accommodation availability claim.
6. **Lake Atitlán:** the traveller can understand stay, visit from an existing base, and deliberate split as different decisions. Split creates only explicitly chosen bases and nights; a simple visit does not create an overnight Lake stop.
7. **Precise route and resilience:** an actionable route skips Discovery. A closed modal resumes after reload under the same owner; Back and Escape preserve drafts; a failed save/commit does not mark the area complete or erase the prompt. Map failure leaves complete card controls. Keyboard, screen reader, reduced-motion, 320–1680 px, and English/Spanish checks pass.

Material risks are content quality and review effort for the Australian threshold; misleading travel claims if route-family evidence is generalized; park-access errors; image rights and missing assets; migration of absent versus explicitly empty #321 choices; partial asynchronous commits; and focus/scroll regressions in a larger dialog. Address these at the stated evidence, persistence, and UI boundaries. Do not lower the coverage or evidence bar merely to make the mockup look full.

## Self-review

This design keeps one capture/brief owner, one canonical place pipeline, one modal shell, and one Builder mutation boundary. Browsing, shortlist, base, visit, and committed stop are distinct. The Australia counts identify separate source, filter, and presentation issues. Mockup facts have not been treated as evidence. Every new persistent field has a read migration and compatibility rule. The scope is Australia first with shared validation paths; it introduces neither a second resolver nor a new routing engine.
